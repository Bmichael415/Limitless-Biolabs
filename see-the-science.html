See the Science

A plain-language look at how these peptides actually work, and why regulatory status varies so much across the category — so you know what you're evaluating.

Mechanisms, briefly

How Each Category Works

Every category on this site works through a distinct biological pathway — they are not interchangeable, and combining categories (stacking) is about targeting more than one pathway at once, not stacking the same effect twice.

GLP-1 / GIP Agonists

Semaglutide, tirzepatide, and retatrutide mimic incretin hormones, slowing gastric emptying and suppressing appetite via receptors in the gut and brain. This is a deficit-driven mechanism — the weight loss comes from eating less, which is why lean-mass loss is a real trade-off without a resistance-training and protein-intake plan.

GH Secretagogues

CJC-1295, Ipamorelin, Sermorelin, and Tesamorelin stimulate the pituitary gland to release growth hormone, raising GH and IGF-1. This pathway is inherently protein-sparing (anabolic) rather than deficit-driven, which is why it tends to preserve or add lean mass instead of depleting it.

Tissue-Repair Peptides

BPC-157 and TB-500 are associated with promoting blood vessel formation (angiogenesis), cell migration to injury sites, and reduced local inflammation — the proposed mechanisms behind their use for tendon, ligament, and soft-tissue recovery.

Mitochondrial & Coenzyme Pathways

NAD+ is a coenzyme essential for mitochondrial ATP production and DNA repair, with levels that decline with age. MOTS-c is encoded in mitochondrial DNA and is associated with how cells respond to metabolic stress — together they're framed as addressing cellular energy from two different angles.

Nootropic Peptides

Semax and Selank are studied for effects on BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor) signaling and stress-hormone regulation, which is the proposed basis for their reported focus and anxiety-reduction effects.

Decision-relevant comparison

GLP-1s vs. GH Secretagogues

Why regulatory status varies

Regulatory Status Matters

Not everything in this category is regulated the same way — knowing which bucket a peptide falls into changes what you should expect and ask for.

FDA-Approved Drugs

Semaglutide, tirzepatide, and tesamorelin have FDA-approved branded versions for specific indications (diabetes, weight management, HIV-associated lipodystrophy). The strongest evidence base in this category.

Compounded Preparations

Versions of approved drugs (and some other peptides) prepared by licensed 503A or 503B pharmacies under their own regulatory framework — including our 503A partner, Stryker Pharmacy — not the same as an FDA-approved commercial product, but produced under pharmacy oversight.

Investigational / Research-Use

Compounds like retatrutide are still in clinical trials and not yet FDA-approved for any use. Many peptides (BPC-157, TB-500, most GH secretagogues, NAD+, Epithalon) are sold as research chemicals with no approved human indication — evidence quality varies widely within this bucket.

What a Certificate of Analysis (COA) actually verifies: a COA confirms the identity and purity of a specific batch — that what's in the vial matches the label and is free of contaminants. It does not, by itself, establish clinical efficacy for a given use. Ask any supplier for the COA on the specific batch you're receiving, not just a general one for the product line.
